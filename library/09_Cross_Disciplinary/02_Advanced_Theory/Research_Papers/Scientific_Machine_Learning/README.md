### 1. In Silico Approach for Predicting Toxicity of Peptides and Proteins

*File: `In_Silico_Approach_for_Predicting_Toxicity_of_Peptides_and_Proteins__W2014274241.pdf`*

**Brief:** Develops in silico models to predict toxicity of peptides and proteins, since toxicity is a bottleneck for peptide-based therapies.

**Main notes:**

- Toxic peptides of 35 or fewer residues were gathered from databases; non-toxic ones from SwissProt and TrEMBL.
- Cys, His, Asn and Pro are abundant and preferred at various positions in toxic peptides.
- Models use machine learning and a quantitative matrix based on peptide properties.

### 2. The advantages of the Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) over F1 score and accuracy in binary classification evaluat

*File: `The_advantages_of_the_Matthews_correlation_coefficient_MCC_over_F1_sco__W2999309192.pdf`*

**Brief:** Argues that MCC is a more reliable metric than accuracy and F1 for binary classification, which can be misleadingly optimistic, especially on imbalanced datasets.

**Main notes:**

- MCC is high only if all four confusion-matrix categories are predicted well.
- Explains MCC's mathematical properties.
- Demonstrated on six synthetic use cases and a real genomics scenario.
