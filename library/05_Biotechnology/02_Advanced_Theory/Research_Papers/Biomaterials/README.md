### 1. Biochar physicochemical properties: pyrolysis temperature and feedstock kind effects

*File: `Biochar_physicochemical_properties_pyrolysis_temperature_and_feedstock__W3004850069.pdf`*

**Brief:** Review of how pyrolysis temperature and feedstock type shape the properties of biochar, a material made by pyrolysis of plant or animal waste.

**Main notes:**

- Applications include pollution remediation, soil fertility improvement and carbon sequestration
- Higher pyrolysis temperature raises surface area, porosity, pH, ash and carbon content but lowers CEC and volatile matter
- Animal litter and solid-waste feedstocks give lower surface area and carbon but higher CEC than crop residues

### 2. Modified Scherrer Equation to Estimate More Accurately Nano-Crystallite Size Using XRD

*File: `Modified_Scherrer_Equation_to_Estimate_More_Accurately_Nano_Crystallit__W2065258689.pdf`*

**Brief:** Proposes a modified Scherrer equation for estimating nano-crystallite size from XRD peak widths. It argues the classic equation gives inconsistent sizes across peaks.

**Main notes:**

- Classic equation (1918) uses peak FWHM, wavelength and a shape factor K around 0.89
- Modified form allows a least-squares fit to minimize error sources
- Argues similar L from all peaks would need implausible peak widths at high angles
